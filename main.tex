\begin{abstract}
    This project is an attempt at creating a \gls{fps} game which is able to create a distinction between the genres of \gls{scifi} and \gls{fantasy}, two sub-genres both covered by the overarching genre Fiction. This paper acts will act as the analysis of the intended effects created within the project and how well they were achieved. This will focus on the specifics of the spells themselves, as well as the various technical components of the project as this reflected my role within said project.

    The project itself also sought to explore alternative control methods within games, with many of similar genres focusing on simple point and click mechanics, this game sought to require the user to actually control their wand in some way in order to cast their spells. This paper will explore how well this was achieved and the various methods used to implement this.
\end{abstract}

\chapter{The Fictional Setting, how setting is defined in a video game} \label{chap1}

\section{An introduction to active storytelling} \label{intro1}

A source of entertainment must be experienced in order for it to entertain. A way in which a given text can be categorised is based on how it entertains the audience. It could either be: passive, in which it provides an experience which does not directly change based on the audiences own thoughts or actions. Or it could be interactive (or active for short). This is where the audience is able to effect the events that occur within the story by making choices \parencite{heinderyckx2013praise}. One of the defining facts of a video game is that it is interactive, the audience is able to interact with the game and assist with its storytelling. This is not to say that all games are required to have a narrative, as some rely on entertainment without a clear story or \gls{todorov}. Whether a narrative is present or not, the feeling and effect of a game is omni-present throughout the gameplay, as it would be throughout a passive medium such as film or literature, every aspect has a direct impact on the feeling that the text creates, let alone its defining characteristics.

As I mentioned earlier, the primary use of a video game is that it is interactive, meaning that the effect can exist as something that is simply experienced such as graphics or audio, but also in the form of the logic behind the game. What the player is able to cause to occur within the game is an good use of the video game medium. This is confirmed time and time again when considering narrative in the context of a video game \parencite{somerdin2016game} \parencite{jones2008narrative} \parencite{lebowitz2011interactive}. As such, we feel that it is essential for us to define the setting within the mechanics of the game itself, rather than simply relying on traditional narrative technologies (the visuals and audio of the game).

\section{The intended effect} \label{goal1}

The setting of \textbf{Archaos} is that of \gls{fantasy}, thus we wished for the games mechanics to reflect this this. We sought the games core gameplay would not make sense in any other setting. That it would be entirely unique to our choice of setting. Our main point of comparison was the \gls{scifi} setting as this represents a future setting, while \gls{fantasy} represents a more historical point of view.

One of the key differences between these 2 settings is the way in which they present innovation and strength. \gls{scifi} puts emphasis on technology, taking things that do or could exist and utilising them for practical benefit (whether the benefit of the individual or the society). It is worth noting that, these technologies and technical innovations are necessitated to hold a sense of plausability. Meaning that the audience must be able to believe that this could be possible within our own reality, and that our current scientific framework could be expanded to include what is presented to us by the text. \parencite{long2011defining}.

In contrast to such, a \gls{fantasy} leans towards magic as a replacement for innovation. As discussed by \cite{carver2024duality} such magic can exist in 2 forms, hard magic or soft magic. Where hard magic is bound by strict rules that are explained clearly to the audience, soft magic is left in the realm of mystery, suggesting that whatever the audience sees of it, is merely a fragment of imagination. Thus it could be said that the difference between the 2 is a matter of approach to magic, or rather, how it is approached by someone who hasn't yet mastered it.

However, with this, there is an issue. As was once said by \cite{clarke1973profiles} ``Any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic.''. This quote highlights an important consideration, how can one differentiate science from magic? If you were to remove any other convention that could be used to understand the setting, how would you tell the difference between magic and technology.

\section{How others have differentiated} \label{bib1}

\subsection{Destiny 2} \label{destiny}

\cite{bungie2017destiny2}

Destiny 2 is an \gls{fps} video game set in a post-apocalyptic future in which a giant ball in the sky (nicknamed ``The Traveller'') is able to resurrect people with magical powers. Due to the futuristic setting, the game makes use of both \gls{scifi} and \gls{fantasy} elements. Furthermore, the game mostly relies on audio to differentiate the 2. Visually they both use similar \gls{vfx} with only a change in colour to denote element. However, the game makes use of very different \gls{timbre} for effects intended to be from the modern setting, as apposed to the more magical audio used during the more mystical actions. This goes to show that audio is a very key part of separating different game settings. As if used properly, multiple settings can even be used in the same situation.

In terms of actual gameplay, a player has guns as their primary combat method, with 4 abilities available on \gls{cooldown}. A grenade, a melee ability, a ``class ability'' and a ``super ability''. All of these make use of some form of magical ability and as such, this is how Destiny 2 creates a separation between the arcane and the scientific within its own gameplay. By simply splitting the uses such that \textit{primary} attack methods are scientific and modern, while \textit{secondary abilities} are used for more fantastical and magical abilities.

\subsection{Witchfire} \label{witchfire}

\cite{astronomicalclub2023witchfire}

Witchfire is an \gls{fps} video game set in a \gls{fantasy} setting in which the pope is sending witch hunters (the player) to hunt witches in distant isles. The game mostly utilises guns with spells being seen as secondary abilities (similar to Destiny 2 in \ref{destiny}). There are 3 primary differences between how the 2 texts present the arcane:

\begin{itemize}

    \item Firstly, Destiny relies on a multi-setting situation to provide the separation while Witchfire fully embraces the medieval setting, as such, all guns have some form of magical ability, while this is only true for some guns in the case of Destiny 2.

    \item Secondly, Destiny 2 divides its abilities into 4 clear categories with clear and distinct mechanics and use cases. A grenade ability is a throwable explosive while a melee ability is applied to a nearby target by making physical contact. However, Witchfire only uses a similar categorisation to determine mechanical impact and \gls{cooldown}, where minor arcana is less powerful but faster to use, while the inverse is true for major arcana.

    \item Finally, major arcana spells are much more mechanically complex. For example, one such spell creates a mystical bell in the air which stuns all nearby enemies when it is shot with a gun (causing it to ring out).

\end{itemize}

Its the final point which intrigues me mostly, as this is what I believe will be more impactful in the long term on a prospective audience. This is what will make a game stand out, rather than the visuals of the magical abilities.

\section{How we differentiated} \label{method1}

Our method for differentiating a \gls{fantasy} setting from a \gls{scifi} setting takes a different approach. Rather than relying on simple casting and mechanical use (such as what is seen in section \ref{witchfire}), we have chosen to focus on a more ideological basis.

The idea of \gls{scifi} is that it is based on a modern approach in which the most trusted method of gathering information and power is that which can be explained using the empirical method. Such a method is typically drawn from maths and logic, ultimately traceable to the underlying concept of cause and effect. \parencite{creath2011logical}. The basis of this idea is therefore that anything with the same cause would have the same effect. As such, the way in which mechanics from a \gls{scifi} setting should be predictable and word as expected. For example, if a button is pressed to fire a gun, the bullet will travel at the same pace, with the same arc, for the same range.

It is then this which allows us to differentiate \gls{scifi} from \gls{fantasy}. As if \gls{scifi} is to represent the concept of logic and order. \gls{fantasy} should represent the antithesis; it should represent chaos and unpredictability. Mechanically this manifests with random effects such as burning, freezing and spawning structures. It can also manifest is large differences between spell. If the game were to use guns, it would act as if 1 gun fired a small bullet, another fired a missile, another would auto track and also explode every 3 seconds during travel. Since we are using spells instead, these must have vastly different mechanics, styles and use cases to showcase the malleability of magic; that a slight change in cause creates a massive change in effect. It is this effect which creates such a sense of chaos, which we feel provides a strong juxtaposition from the order that would appear in more futuristic settings.

Furthermore, chaos can also be represented through a scientific approach known as \gls{chaostheory} \parencite{lorenz2004deterministic}. This is a theory which suggests that small changes to a system can lead to drastic changes in results. There are a few ways in which this could be implemented within a gaming environment. The most \textit{programmatic} of which would be the use of chain reactions, where events can trigger other events which trigger other events. Ultimately culminating in a very different situation if only 1 of the earlier events are not triggered.

Another useful difference of which we were able to exploit is the difference in plausibility between \gls{scifi} and \gls{fantasy}. \gls{scifi} is ultimately set in the future, and thus there is an expectation that it would be based upon current technologies. In any case, \gls{scifi} is expected to stay within the realms of possibilty, as if it could happen in the future at some point. However, \gls{fantasy} is not bound by the same rules. Since \gls{fantasy} is set in a historical setting, it can be proven to have not happened. It is not necessary for it to hold the same suspension of disbelief and thus can completely subvert natural laws and empirically proven facts as it is already proven to exist outside of known reality. \parencite{long2011defining}

\section{Separating order from chaos} \label{results1}

Largely, the intention described in \ref{method1} is successful. However, there is 1 key factor that is not considered. The \textit{fun} factor. You see, within our description, we analysed how to create a believable setting, but did not yet consider how this equates to creating a fun game. This is incredibly problematic for a video game especially as these thrive almost purely on the existence of fun within the text.

As can be seen in many recent submissions to the medium (\cite{bungie2017destiny2} and \cite{ea2017battlefront2} \footnote{it is worth noting that much of the distaste towards this games use of randomness is from their \textit{lootbox} system which caused young children to essentially gamble money to gain mechanical benefits for use within the game.}), overuse of randomisation within a game can rapidly reduce the \textit{fun} created by a game. This relates back to the original definition defined in \ref{intro1}. In this we stated that the purpose of the video game medium is that the player is able to make choices which directly impact what goes on within the game, the text is in their control. By introducing randomness, you force the player to relinquish some level of control to the text itself, thus bringing into question the purpose of the text being a game in the first place. As such, randomness should be used sparingly within a video game to enable the player to have some level of control over their actions.

\section{Conclusion} \label{conclusion1}

Ultimately, the use of chaos within the mechanics of a game is largely down to a choice of style as well as a management of implementation. By utilising \gls{chaostheory}, one can implement a chaotic, random system without directly removing control from the player. And small amounts of randomness can enhance the experience without diminishing it entirely.
